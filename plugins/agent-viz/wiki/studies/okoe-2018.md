# Okoe, Jianu & Kobourov (2018/2019), *Node-Link or Adjacency Matrices*

Mmumbi Okoe, Radu Jianu and Stephen Kobourov, "Node-Link or Adjacency Matrices: Old Question, New Insights," *IEEE TVCG* 25(10), 2019, pp. 2940-2952. Expanded from a Graph Drawing 2017 paper. A large crowdsourced re-examination of the question [Ghoniem et al.](ghoniem-2004.md) answered in 2004.

**How this was read.** Accepted version from the City, University of London open-access repository, text re-extracted locally. This is the accepted manuscript, not the published version.

**What it is good for.** The counter-evidence. It is the reason "matrices beat node-link above twenty nodes" cannot be stated flatly.

**What it does not settle.** The general question. The authors are explicit that their findings apply to networks like theirs with an interaction set like theirs.

---

## What changed from 2004

Three things, and all three plausibly drive the different answer:

| | Ghoniem et al. (2004) | Okoe et al. (2018) |
|---|---|---|
| Networks | Random, 20-100 nodes, density 0.2-0.6 | Two real networks: 256 nodes / 1,090 edges, and 332 nodes / 2,126 edges |
| Display | Static | Interactive |
| Tasks | 7 | 14, spanning multiple task taxonomies |
| Participants | Lab, volunteers | 864 online |

The authors describe the difference in their own terms: "Ghoniem et al. evaluated random networks with fewer nodes, albeit somewhat denser."

That understates it slightly. Their networks are larger *and* roughly an order of magnitude sparser, which is the regime most real networks live in.

## Results

From the introduction:

> "NL outperforms AM for questions about graph topology (e.g., 'Select all neighbors of node,' 'Is a highlighted node connected to a named node?'). Of 10 such tasks in two datasets, participants who used the node-link diagram were more accurate in 5 and less accurate in 3. AM outperforms NL in 2 of 4 tasks which tested the ability of the participants to identify and compare node groups or clusters. Finally, NL has a slight edge on 1 of 2 memorability tasks."

And the conclusion:

> "We found that NL is better than AM for questions about network topology, connectivity, and memorability tasks, while AM outperforms NL for group tasks. These findings apply to visualizing datasets similar to the ones we evaluated, provided a similar interaction set."

**The counts behind the headline.** "NL outperforms AM for topology" summarizes 5 wins, 3 losses and 2 ties out of 10. That is a real effect and a modest one. The group-task result for matrices is 2 of 4. Neither representation sweeps.

## Reconciling the two studies

They are not in direct contradiction, and pretending one refuted the other misreports both.

- **Density.** Ghoniem tested 0.2 to 0.6. Okoe's networks sit near 0.03. Node-link's failure mode is edge occlusion, which is a function of density. At Ghoniem's densities a 100-node node-link diagram is a hairball; at Okoe's it is legible.
- **Interaction.** Ghoniem's displays were static, and their own conclusion asks for interaction on path tasks. Okoe supplied it. Interaction disproportionately rescues node-link, because the thing it fixes is exactly occlusion and tracing.
- **Task set.** Okoe's 14 tasks include grouping and memorability, which 2004 did not test. Matrices win the grouping tasks, consistent with the 2004 finding that ordered matrices support structure reading.

The defensible synthesis: **matrices are favored by density and by group-structure tasks; node-link is favored by sparsity, by interaction, and by topology and path tasks.** Size alone, which is how the 2004 result is usually quoted, is not the variable that decides it.

## Limits the authors state

- Results apply to "the specific underlying networks and the specific implementations of NL and AM visualizations."
- Some participants reported insufficient time. The authors argue both conditions were affected equally, so comparisons are likely unaffected.
- Two networks. Real, but two.

## Evidence class

- **Evidence-backed, within scope.** On sparse real-world networks of a few hundred nodes with interaction, node-link is better for topology, connectivity and memorability; matrices are better for group and cluster tasks.
- **Contested, jointly with 2004.** Any general "X beats Y for large graphs" claim. The two studies together support a *conditional* answer and refute the unconditional one.
